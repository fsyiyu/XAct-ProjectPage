Abstract

X-Act decomposes action transfer into action tracing, target-scene motion planning, and target video generation — all realized within a single video diffusion transformer.

Action transfer must preserve action semantics — trajectory, contact timing, and object response — while discarding source appearance such as hand shape, skin, and scene texture. Existing approaches either inject motion features that are entangled with the source actor, causing severe identity leakage, or rely on dense per-frame masks and trajectories that cannot adapt to a new embodiment. Both paradigms skip the intermediate step of understanding the demonstrated action before planning how it should unfold in the target scene.

X-Act encodes the reference video as a clean Trace segment, predicts a dense optical-flow segment as a target-scene Flow motion plan during denoising, and generates the Target video from the target anchor frame under the joint guidance of Trace and Flow. Three designs support this motion chain: the Trace–Flow Motion Planner turns planning into explicit flow prediction; Segment-Gapped RoPE (SG-RoPE) inserts learnable positional gaps between segments; and Trace-Anchored Causal Attention (TACA) enforces a unidirectional Trace → Flow → Target information flow.

Trained on X-Act-30K — 30,000 action-matched video pairs covering 107 manipulation categories — X-Act achieves leading results on a 200-case test bed (80 H2H / 80 R2R / 40 H2R) and in real-robot deployment, without requiring dense inference-time annotations.

Method

Trace–Flow Motion Planner

Motion planning is realized as dense optical-flow prediction inside the denoising process: only Flow and Target are noised during training, with RAFT flow as supervision, making the plan an explicit and decodable intermediate stage.

Segment-Gapped RoPE (SG-RoPE)

The three segments share one monotonic timeline: within each segment, frames are numbered continuously, while learnable gaps between segments prevent boundaries from being read as continuous physical motion.

Trace-Anchored Causal Attention (TACA)

An asymmetric causal attention mask: Trace attends only to itself; Flow does not look back at Target; Target can attend to everything — action understanding, motion planning, and visual realization flow in one direction.
